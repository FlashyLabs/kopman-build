01 — The Group

A builder that
stopped selling its margin.

Most construction firms convert skill into a fee, hand over the keys, and start again at zero on the next job. Kopman Group is structured so that the same skill can convert into something the group keeps.

The group began as a construction business and still is one. Kopman Build has worked across the Greater Toronto Area and Muskoka since 1999 — custom homes, full-house renovations, additions, laneway houses and design-build delivery. That business carries the licences, the trade relationships, the estimating discipline and the track record that everything else here borrows credibility from.

What has changed is what happens to the capability once a project ends. Kopman Properties acquires buildings that need precisely the work Kopman Build performs. BuildOS — software the group wrote for its own operations — runs both. Each company is a supplier to, or a customer of, the others.

The operating claim is narrow and testable: a group that can design, permit, build, own and operate a property internally does not pay anyone else to do those things. Every fee a conventional developer pays out — general contractor, project manager, construction manager — is a fee this group pays to itself, and can therefore convert into equity in the asset rather than expense against it.

That is the entire thesis. Everything on this page is its consequence.

03 — The Model

Where the same crew
ends up.

A construction business and a property business look similar from the outside — both put crews on sites. They are not similar. The difference is what the group owns when the crew drives away.

Cost basis, not quoted price

Construction scope enters an acquisition model at internal cost. A competing buyer models the same building at a general contractor's tendered price. On identical assumptions, this group can bid higher and still clear its return — or bid level and clear more.

Certainty of execution

The largest risk in repositioning a building is that the work overruns. This group is not estimating a stranger's ability to deliver. It is scheduling its own crews, against its own historical cost data, on scope it performs every week.

Two revenue shapes

Construction revenue is lumpy, won job by job, and stops when the phone stops. Property income is contracted and recurring. The group is deliberately building the second on top of the first.

05 — Standards

How we
hold ourselves.

A group that builds for itself has to be harder on itself than a client would be. These are the rules that make internal work honest.

01

Internal work is priced at arm's length

Every contract between group companies carries a written scope and a market benchmark. "Built at cost" is an advantage only if the cost is real — absorbing an overrun in one company to flatter another relocates a loss rather than avoiding one.

02

We publish what we can substantiate

Figures we put in front of a partner, a vendor or a lender are ones we can produce the working for. Where a number is an estimate, we say so on the page rather than in a footnote.

03

Conditions before commitments

Environmental assessment, structural review and zoning confirmation are conditions of purchase, not post-closing discoveries. No exception is made because a process is competitive.

04

Capability before expansion

A new arm opens when there is a completed track record to open it with. Kopman Capital waits for finished repositionings; BuildOS is licensed externally only after it has run the group's own projects end to end.

05

One trade relationship, many projects

The subcontractors who have worked with Kopman Build for two decades are the reason schedules hold. They are paid on time, on every project, including the group's own.

06

The group is answerable for all of it

A client of one company is a client of the group. There is one place to escalate and one set of people accountable, whichever name is on the contract.
