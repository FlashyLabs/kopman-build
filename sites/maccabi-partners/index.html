01  ·  Thesis

Technology should make
good businesses better,
not make them obsolete.

Most technology capital funds the company trying to disintermediate an industry. We fund the industry, and give it the tools.

Disruption creates value by destroying something. A disruptive company takes share that belonged to an incumbent — the gain on one side of the ledger is a loss on the other, and the loss lands on real firms and real employment. That trade is often worth making. It is also only one of two available paths.

Non-disruptive creation is the other. It opens markets that did not exist, or raises the productivity of markets that already do, without taking share from anyone. The term is not ours: it is the thesis of Beyond Disruption by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, the Blue Ocean Strategy authors, published by Harvard Business Review Press in 2023.

We apply it to a narrow and unglamorous field — software and systems that make established operating businesses better at work they already do well. Estimating, scheduling, cost intelligence, procurement, quality control.

Not marketplaces that cut out the contractor. Not platforms that turn a trade into a commodity. Tools the operator owns, and whose gains the operator keeps.

It is also a commercial position, not only an ethical one. An owner deciding who to sell thirty years of work to cares a great deal whether the buyer intends to build on it or dismantle it.

02  ·  Structure

Two families,
one vehicle.

Maccabi Partners is jointly owned by Gord Holdings and Kopman Holdings, and was seeded from both houses. It is a holding company, not a fund — no blind pool, no management fee and no term.

What permanent capital means in practice

A private equity fund has a term. It raises capital, deploys it, and must return it — usually within about five years of an investment, whether or not that is the right moment for the business.

We have no term, because we have no outside investors to return capital to. That means we can hold a good business indefinitely, invest through a slow year without explaining it to anyone, and tell a seller honestly that we are not planning to sell what we are asking them to hand over.

It also means we move at the pace of two principals rather than a deal team. We buy few things, and we look at them closely.

Where GDA Group ends and Maccabi begins

GDA Group is Michael Gord's firm and its stated position is global capital for disruptive technology. Maccabi is its precise complement, and the boundary is the thesis itself:

If a business wins by displacing an incumbent, it is not a Maccabi investment. If it wins by making an incumbent better, it is not a GDA one.

Between them the two vehicles cover the whole innovation spectrum without ever bidding against each other for the same opportunity.

03  ·  Mandate

What we buy —
and what we decline.

Maccabi holds a balance sheet seeded from both families, spanning several sectors. What it originates is narrower, and these criteria govern that book.

We originate
  • Owner-operated businesses in construction, property, trades and adjacent services
  • Companies with real revenue and real customers — not pre-revenue technology
  • Situations where a software or systems layer measurably raises the operator's margin
  • Southern Ontario, where we can be on site rather than on a call
  • Owners who care where their business and their people end up
  • Control or meaningful minority positions, held without a defined exit date
We decline
  • Technology whose business model requires an existing industry to shrink
  • Marketplaces that disintermediate the operator we would otherwise back
  • Pre-revenue or pre-product companies — that is GDA Group's remit, not ours
  • Anything that requires a five-year exit to work
  • Businesses neither of us could stand in front of and explain
  • Competitive processes where we would be one bidder among many

A note on the two books. Maccabi was seeded at formation with contributed positions from both houses, including holdings across sectors outside this mandate. Those predate the mandate, and they are the reason the firm has seen more than one industry. They are held and managed. What Maccabi goes out and acquires from here is governed by the criteria above, without exception.

04  ·  Approach

Four levers,
in this order.

The discipline is what we refuse to underwrite. Nothing in the fourth is ever assumed at the point we decide to buy.

01

Acquire well

We buy owner-operated businesses in a market where continuity is worth more to the seller than the last increment of price. We are not the highest bidder, and we do not try to be.

02

Operate better

We apply estimating, scheduling and cost discipline that already exists inside Kopman Group — not a consultant's framework, but the systems a working construction business has refined since 1999.

03

Equip with software

We deploy tooling built and proven on our own job sites before it is offered to anyone else. A platform its owner does not run is a slide, not a product.

04

Re-rate at scale — never underwritten

Consolidating small operators into one professionalised platform tends to attract a higher multiple. It is the most cited driver of returns in this strategy and the least controllable. We treat it as an outcome we may enjoy, never as an assumption a deal depends on. Any transaction that only works on this lever is one we decline.

05  ·  Principals

Two people.
Both on the hook.

A firm this size is its principals. Both invest their own capital, and both have other roles we state plainly rather than obscure.

Dustin Kopman

Partner · Operations

Founder of Kopman Group, a Greater Toronto Area construction and property business he has run since 1999 — custom homes, renovations and design-build across the GTA and Muskoka, and more recently the acquisition and repositioning of industrial property.

He brings the part of this that cannot be hired: two and a half decades of knowing which trades turn up, what a job actually costs, and which businesses in this market are worth owning.

Also: founder, Kopman Group — in which Maccabi Partners holds equity.

Michael Gord

Partner · Capital

Founder of GDA Group, a merchant bank and capital markets firm positioned around disruptive technology, with a career spent building and financing technology companies.

He brings the capital structure, the transaction discipline and the technology judgement — and, in Maccabi, a deliberate counterweight to his own day job: a mandate defined by everything GDA is not.

Also: founder, GDA Group — which holds a separate and non-overlapping mandate.

Maccabi invests in businesses its principals also own or operate. Related-party transactions are contracted at arm's length against an external benchmark, and the potential conflicts are stated on this page rather than disclosed on request.
