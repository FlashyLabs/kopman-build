Second Storey Addition Bloor West Village

Bloor West Village's 1930s–1950s brick homes and bungalows, set along quiet streets feeding off the vibrant commercial strip on Bloor, leave growing families with a clear choice: add a second storey or leave a neighbourhood they love. Why Bloor West Village families choose Kopman Build

Bloor West Village is one of Toronto's most sought-after family neighbourhoods — anchored by the vibrant commercial strip on Bloor Street West and defined by its tight-knit streets of solid brick homes built between the 1930s and 1950s. Those homes were designed for smaller households; a second storey addition is the most direct way to gain the bedrooms and bathrooms a growing family needs without leaving a neighbourhood they love.

Adding a second storey to a pre-war bungalow demands real structural knowledge. The existing floor system, load-bearing walls, and sometimes the foundation all need assessment before a single framing member goes up. Kopman Build coordinates a structural engineer on every project and brings in the masonry trades needed to match the brick detailing that defines this neighbourhood's character.

Dustin Kopman founded the company in 1999 after years working in construction through his family's firm, G&G Enterprise. That hands-on background means every Bloor West Village project is overseen by someone who understands brick construction, Toronto zoning, and the Committee of Adjustment process — not just someone who manages subcontractors at arm's length.

Second storey addition scope in Bloor West Village

Structural Assessment

Engineer-led review of existing foundations, bearing walls, and floor systems before any design is finalized — essential on 1930s–1950s bungalows.

Full Second Storey

Complete new storey above the existing footprint — primary bedroom, additional bedrooms, bathrooms, and hallway, fully integrated with the home below.

Brick & Masonry Matching

Sourcing and laying brick that matches the existing Bloor West Village character — no jarring material changes on the exterior.

Permit Management

Full City of Toronto permit application, Committee of Adjustment coordination if required, and all inspection milestones handled by Kopman Build.

Interior Integration

New staircase, updated HVAC, electrical panel upgrades, and plumbing rough-ins — the full scope of work behind the walls, not just framing and drywall.

Dormer Additions

Partial second-storey solutions using dormers to add headroom and usable space where a full addition is not warranted or not permitted.

Questions about second storey additions in Bloor West Village

How much does a second storey addition cost in Bloor West Village?

A second storey addition on a Bloor West Village bungalow typically runs $350,000–$650,000+, depending on the footprint, structural scope, and finish level. Many 1930s–1950s homes in the neighbourhood require beam and foundation upgrades to carry the new load, which is factored into our pre-construction estimate. We provide a detailed line-item estimate before any commitment.

Can my 1930s or 1940s Bloor West Village bungalow support a second storey?

Yes, in most cases — but a structural assessment is required first. The solid brick construction typical of Bloor West Village homes from this era often provides a good foundation, though the floor structure, load-bearing walls, and sometimes the foundation may need reinforcing. Kopman Build coordinates a structural engineer as part of the pre-construction process to determine exactly what is needed.

Do I need a permit for a second storey addition in Toronto?

Yes. All second storey additions in Toronto require a building permit from the City of Toronto. Depending on your lot and zoning, a Committee of Adjustment application for a minor variance may also be required. In heritage-adjacent areas of Bloor West Village, there may be additional design considerations. Kopman Build manages the full permit and approval process on your behalf.

How long does a second storey addition take in Bloor West Village?

From initial consultation to final occupancy permit, expect 14–20 months in total. Permitting and design typically account for 4–8 months, and construction runs 8–12 months depending on scope. We give you a realistic schedule at the outset and keep you updated throughout — no surprises.
