Basement Renovation High Park

High Park's surrounding streets are lined with 1910s–1940s homes — Edwardian and interwar houses with low basement ceilings, stone foundations, and limited natural light. Kopman Build specializes in transforming these older basements into legal secondary suites, finished family rooms, and home offices, navigating the structural and permit complexities that come with century-old construction.

Questions about basement renovation High Park

How much does a basement renovation cost in High Park?

Basement renovation in High Park typically costs $65,000–$140,000 for a standard finish. The 1910s–1940s homes common to this neighbourhood often require additional work — waterproofing aging stone or block foundations, upgrading knob-and-tube wiring, and underpinning for low ceilings — which can add $30,000–$80,000+ to the overall budget. We provide detailed, line-item estimates before any commitment.

Do older High Park homes need underpinning before the basement can be finished?

Many of the pre-war homes around High Park have basement ceiling heights below the 1.95m minimum required for a legal secondary suite, and below the 2.1m–2.2m most owners want for a comfortable living space. Underpinning or bench-pinning can increase ceiling height by 12–24 inches. Kopman Build assesses your existing foundation and recommends the right approach before any finishing work begins.

Can I create a legal secondary suite in my High Park home?

Yes. High Park falls within the City of Toronto's zoning that permits second suites in most residential properties. Your basement must meet Ontario Building Code minimums for ceiling height, egress windows, fire separation, and dedicated heating. Given the age of the housing stock in the area, most projects also require foundation waterproofing and electrical upgrades. Kopman Build handles permitting, inspections, and all required code work from start to finish.

How long does a basement renovation take in a High Park home?

A standard basement finish in a High Park home takes 10–16 weeks. If underpinning is required to increase ceiling height, add 4–8 weeks before finishing can begin. A full legal secondary suite — including plumbing, fire separation, egress windows, and mechanical — typically runs 14–20 weeks. Older homes often surface unexpected conditions during demolition; we build contingency time into our schedules accordingly.
