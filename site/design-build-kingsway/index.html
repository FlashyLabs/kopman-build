Design-Build Kingsway

The Kingsway's 1920s and 1940s custom homes — set on generous lots above the Humber River ravine — demand a level of craft and coordination that only a unified design-build team can reliably deliver. Kopman Build manages design, permits, and construction as a single engagement, protecting the architectural character that defines one of Etobicoke's most prestigious enclaves.

Why Kingsway clients choose Kopman Build

Homes in the Kingsway are among Etobicoke's most architecturally considered — Tudor Revival, English Cottage, and Georgian Revival houses built between the wars, on lots that frequently extend to ravine edges or back onto Humber River greenway. Renovating them well is not a standard exercise. Heritage-adjacent detailing, older structural systems, TRCA oversight for ravine properties, and neighbours and streetscapes that set a high visual standard all require design and construction to function as one coordinated body — not two firms managing a handoff.

Dustin Kopman founded the company in 1999 after growing up working in construction through his family's firm, G&G Enterprise. The design-build model we use today came directly from observing what goes wrong when design and construction are separated: cost overruns traced to design decisions made without real construction data, schedule delays caused by permit re-submissions, and finger-pointing that leaves clients caught in the middle. We eliminated those failure points by bringing both sides under one roof.

What makes the Kingsway different

Ravine and TRCA oversight. A significant portion of Kingsway lots adjoin the Humber River ravine system, placing them within TRCA regulated areas. Any addition, grading change, or structure near the ravine edge requires a separate TRCA permit alongside City of Toronto building permits. Our design-build model includes full management of this dual-track approval process — we have done it before and know what each authority needs to see.

Period architecture requires period-literate design. The Kingsway's houses were built by craftsmen working in established historical traditions. Additions and renovations that ignore that context — or that apply contemporary minimalism without sensitivity to the original form — reduce both the property's appeal and its long-term value. Our design partners are selected for their ability to work within and extend existing architectural vocabulary, not override it.

Lot size creates genuine scope for major projects. Kingsway lots are substantially larger than those found in most Toronto neighbourhoods. That means rear additions, carriage house conversions, garden suites, and full second-storey additions are often feasible where they would not be elsewhere. We begin every engagement with a zoning and coverage analysis so clients understand what is actually possible on their specific lot before any design investment is made.

Questions about design-build in Kingsway

What makes design-build a good fit for Kingsway homes?

Kingsway homes from the 1920s and 1940s are architecturally distinctive — Tudor Revival, English Cottage, and Georgian styles on generous ravine-adjacent lots. Renovating them well requires design and construction to work as one. The design-build model lets us resolve heritage-adjacent detailing, structural surprises in older homes, and Etobicoke's permit requirements under a single coordinated team, which is faster and produces better results than splitting design and construction across separate firms.

Can you handle additions to Kingsway homes that back onto the Humber River ravine?

Yes — ravine-adjacent properties in Etobicoke are subject to TRCA (Toronto and Region Conservation Authority) review in addition to standard City of Toronto permits. Our design-build model includes full management of TRCA submissions and any required geotechnical or environmental assessments. We coordinate all regulatory approvals as part of the pre-construction phase, so there are no surprises when the project starts.

How does Kopman Build approach full custom renovations on larger Kingsway lots?

Larger lots in the Kingsway often allow for substantial additions — rear extensions, second-storey additions, or ancillary structures — that would not be feasible on smaller Toronto properties. We begin every engagement with a zoning and lot coverage analysis so clients understand what is possible before committing to design fees. Our design-build process then develops a scheme that maximizes the lot's potential while respecting the neighbourhood's architectural character.

What does a design-build project in Kingsway typically cost?

Whole-home renovations in the Kingsway — given the scale of properties and the calibre of finishes typical in Etobicoke's premium enclaves — commonly range from $400,000 to well over $1,000,000 depending on scope. Additions can range from $300,000 for a modest rear addition to $700,000+ for a full second-storey or complex ravine-adjacent build. We provide a detailed line-item estimate with allowances and contingencies before any commitment, so cost certainty is established from the design phase.
