Renovation Contractor Danforth Village

Danforth Village's 1920s and 1940s semis and bungalows are ideally suited to second-storey additions and open-concept kitchen renovations — the two projects that define this family neighbourhood's renovation market. Kopman Build has delivered these projects along the Danforth corridor from Greektown to Woodbine for over 25 years.

Why Danforth Village families choose Kopman Build

The Danforth Village housing stock is wonderfully consistent — brick semis and detached bungalows built between the wars, with generous lot depths and tight party-wall relationships that demand a contractor who understands pre-war construction. Knob-and-tube wiring, cast-iron drains, and balloon-framed walls are the norm, not the exception. Kopman Build's crews know these buildings and plan around their realities from day one. Three things define renovation work here:

  • Family renovation market. Danforth Village attracts families who need more space without leaving the neighbourhood. Additions and second storeys are the dominant project type — not cosmetic refreshes.
  • Second-storey additions on bungalows. Adding a full upper floor to a 1930s bungalow is technically demanding — it involves structural assessment, engineered beams, a City of Toronto permit, and careful sequencing to keep the home liveable during construction.
  • Kitchen opens on semis and bungalows. The main floors in these homes were designed for a different era. Removing load-bearing walls to connect the kitchen to the living and dining area is the renovation that transforms daily life — and it's one of the projects we know best.

Questions about renovation contractor Danforth Village

How much does a second-storey addition cost on a Danforth Village bungalow?

A second-storey addition on a typical 1920s–1940s Danforth Village bungalow generally runs $250,000–$450,000+, depending on the footprint, structural conditions, and finish level. The existing foundation and framing are assessed during pre-construction to identify any remediation work before pricing is finalized.

Do you need a permit for a second-storey addition in Danforth Village?

Yes. Second-storey additions require a building permit from the City of Toronto, along with structural engineering drawings and a zoning review. Many Danforth Village properties also fall under Toronto's Zoning By-law 569-2013, which governs lot coverage and setbacks. Kopman Build manages the full permit process from application through final inspection.

What is a kitchen open-concept renovation on a semi-detached home?

A kitchen open on a Danforth Village semi typically involves removing the wall between the kitchen and the rear living or dining space to create a connected, open layout. Because many of these walls are load-bearing, the work requires a structural engineer, a permit, and an LVL or steel beam to carry the load. The result is a significantly more functional main floor — the most requested renovation in the neighbourhood.

How long does a whole-home renovation take on a 1930s Danforth Village semi?

Whole-home renovations on pre-war Danforth Village semis typically run 6–12 months depending on scope. Knob-and-tube electrical, cast-iron plumbing, and balloon framing are common in this housing stock and are factored into the project plan during pre-construction. We build realistic schedules and update you weekly throughout construction.

Does Kopman Build work on both semis and detached bungalows in Danforth Village?

Yes. We regularly work on both semi-detached homes and detached bungalows throughout the Danforth corridor from Greektown to Woodbine. Each building type has its own structural considerations — shared party walls on semis, wider rear yards on detached bungalows — and we tailor our approach accordingly.
