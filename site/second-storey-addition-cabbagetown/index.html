Second Storey Addition Cabbagetown

Cabbagetown is home to the largest intact Victorian residential district in North America — 1880s and 1910s row houses that reward careful, heritage-sensitive renovation. Kopman Build has been adding second storeys to Toronto homes since 1999, working within the City's heritage conservation requirements to double your living space without compromising the character that makes Cabbagetown exceptional.

Why Cabbagetown clients choose Kopman Build

Adding a second storey in Cabbagetown is more involved than a standard Toronto addition. The neighbourhood sits within a City of Toronto Heritage Conservation District, meaning exterior alterations require a Heritage Permit alongside the standard Building Permit. Kopman Build coordinates both approval streams — working with heritage-experienced architects to meet HCD guidelines while maximising the interior program you actually want.

Interior renovations in Cabbagetown are straightforward and not subject to heritage review — which means a well-planned second-storey addition can deliver completely modern living space above a preserved Victorian streetscape. We have navigated this combination repeatedly and know exactly where the design constraints sit and where they do not.

Cabbagetown row houses from the 1880s and 1910s also vary considerably in structural condition. We arrange a structural engineer assessment early to confirm feasibility and define any foundation or framing reinforcement before design dollars are spent — a step many contractors skip until it becomes a costly surprise mid-construction.

Questions about second storey additions in Cabbagetown

Do I need a heritage permit to add a second storey to my Cabbagetown home?

Most likely yes. Cabbagetown is a City of Toronto Heritage Conservation District, which means any exterior alterations — including a second-storey addition — require a Heritage Permit from the City in addition to a standard Building Permit. Interior work does not trigger heritage review. Kopman Build manages both permit streams and works with heritage-experienced architects to meet the HCD guidelines.

How much does a second storey addition cost on a Cabbagetown Victorian row house?

A second-storey addition on a Cabbagetown Victorian row house typically runs $350,000–$650,000+, depending on the footprint, finish level, and heritage detailing required. Heritage-compatible exterior materials and custom millwork to match existing Victorian profiles add cost relative to a standard addition. We provide a detailed pre-construction estimate before any commitment.

How long does a second storey addition take in Cabbagetown?

Plan for 10–16 months total from project start to occupancy. Heritage permit review adds 4–8 weeks on top of the standard building permit timeline. Construction itself runs 8–12 months depending on scope and finish. We build a realistic schedule at the outset and track it weekly.

Can a Cabbagetown Victorian row house structurally support a second storey addition?

Many can, but it depends on the condition of the existing foundation and main floor structure. Cabbagetown row houses from the 1880s–1910s vary considerably in structural condition. Kopman Build arranges a structural engineer assessment early in the process to confirm feasibility and define any reinforcement scope before design or permits begin.
