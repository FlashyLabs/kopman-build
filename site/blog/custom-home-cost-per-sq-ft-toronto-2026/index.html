Custom home cost per square foot in Toronto

Real numbers from 25 years of building in this city — what you will actually spend in 2026, what moves that number up or down, and how to build a budget that holds.

The number everyone asks first

The first question almost every client asks is some version of: "What does it cost per square foot?" It is a reasonable question. It gives you a ballpark to decide whether a project is even viable before you commit to months of planning. The problem is that the answer depends heavily on what you are building, where you are building it, and what level of finish you expect. A per-square-foot figure without those qualifiers is nearly meaningless.

That said, you need a starting point. Based on our 2026 project experience in Toronto and the GTA, here is the honest range: custom home construction in Toronto currently runs from approximately $380 per square foot on the low end to $650 per square foot at mid-range, with high-specification and luxury builds regularly reaching $800 to $1,100 per square foot or higher. These figures cover construction cost only. They do not include land, soft costs, or HST.

If you have seen numbers quoted lower than $380 per square foot for a custom home in Toronto in 2026, something is being left out of the calculation, the specification level is very basic, or you are being given a number designed to win your attention rather than accurately reflect the project.

The sections below explain what drives costs within these ranges, how to read a budget breakdown, and what to watch for when you are comparing estimates from different builders.

What the per-square-foot number does not include

Before going further into the construction cost breakdown, it is worth being specific about what the per-square-foot figure typically excludes, because these items are often substantial and are frequently left out of initial estimates to make a number look more palatable.

  • Land: In Toronto proper, serviced residential lots are exceptionally scarce. A lot in Leaside, Lawrence Park, or Rosedale that can support a new custom home typically costs $1.5M to $3M+, depending on size and zoning. In the inner suburbs — North York, Etobicoke, East York — expect $900K to $1.8M for a lot suitable for a larger new build. This is your biggest single cost and is entirely separate from construction.
  • Soft costs: Architectural and engineering fees, permit fees, development charges, surveys, geotechnical reports, and legal costs typically add 12–18% to your construction budget. On a $1.5M build, that is $180K to $270K in costs that never touch the physical building.
  • HST: New home construction in Ontario is subject to HST. The federal-provincial rebate applies to homes under certain value thresholds, but on a custom home above $450K (which is nearly all of them in Toronto), you are looking at an effective HST rate of approximately 13% on most of the construction value. Budget for it explicitly.
  • Site work and servicing: Demolition of an existing structure, excavation, tree removal, utility connections, and grading are often quoted separately. These can add $80K to $200K+ depending on site conditions.
  • Landscaping and exterior finishes: Driveway, walkways, fencing, soft landscaping, and deck or patio work are frequently excluded from base construction quotes and represent another $50K to $150K depending on the scope.

The full delivered cost of a custom home in Toronto — land, soft costs, construction, HST, and site work — is typically 1.4 to 1.6 times the construction budget alone. Understand this multiplier before you assess whether a project makes sense for your situation.

Cost tiers: what you get at each level

Within the construction budget itself, specification choices drive costs more than almost anything else. Here is how the major tiers break down in practice.

Tier Cost Per Sq Ft (2026) Typical Profile
Production-level custom $320–$420 Builder-grade cabinetry, standard fixtures, vinyl or engineered flooring, conventional framing and mechanical systems. Limited customization. Often a semi-custom model with pre-set plan options.
Mid-range custom $420–$600 Full architectural design, hardwood or tile flooring throughout, solid-wood cabinetry, mid-grade appliances, conventional HVAC with HRV, 9–10 ft ceilings, quality windows and exterior cladding.
Upper-mid custom $600–$800 Higher-end finishes throughout, custom millwork and cabinetry, stone countertops and tile work, more complex architectural detailing, better window packages (triple-pane), smart home integration, quality landscaping included.
Luxury / high-specification $800–$1,100+ Premium everything: imported stone, bespoke millwork, radiant floor heating throughout, high-performance building envelope, full home automation, elevator, wine room, theatre. Complex design with extensive custom work in every room.

Most of Kopman Build's work sits in the upper-mid to luxury range. The clients we serve are not looking for the minimum viable house — they are building a home they intend to live in for 20 or 30 years, and they want it done properly the first time. That changes how we approach specification decisions and where we counsel clients to spend versus save.

Where the money actually goes

Understanding the construction budget requires understanding which trades and line items carry the most weight. Here is a representative budget breakdown for a mid-range custom home in Toronto at approximately $500 per square foot, assuming a 3,000 sq ft house above grade.

Budget Category Approx. % of Hard Costs Notes
Foundation & structure 12–15% Excavation, concrete, waterproofing, structural framing. Toronto's clay soils often require deeper foundations and more extensive waterproofing than many clients anticipate.
Exterior envelope 10–14% Roofing, windows and doors, cladding, exterior insulation. Window specification alone can vary by $40K to $120K+ between builder-grade and high-performance packages.
Rough mechanical (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) 14–18% Rough-in for all trades before walls close. This is the work you never see but absolutely must get right. Cutting corners here has consequences that show up years later.
Insulation & drywall 5–7% Includes spray foam at rim joists and critical details, batt or blown insulation, vapour barrier, and all drywall, taping, and finishing.
Flooring 6–10% Varies dramatically by material choice. Engineered hardwood is a mid-point; solid hardwood, large-format tile, and heated tile floors all push the number higher.
Cabinetry & millwork 10–16% The single most variable line item in most projects. A mid-range kitchen cabinet package starts around $35K; custom painted cabinetry throughout the home can reach $150K to $250K+.
Countertops, tile & stone 4–8% Quartz countertops are the mid-range default; natural stone and bookmatch applications push costs up significantly. Bathroom tile selections can also move this number materially.
Fixtures & finishes 5–9% Plumbing fixtures, light fixtures, hardware. Mid-range fixture allowances are often underestimated. Clients frequently upgrade once they see the actual options.
Stairs, doors & trim 4–7% A custom open-riser staircase with glass panels is a significant cost item. Solid-core doors, quality hardware, and detailed trim carpentry add up quickly.
General conditions & overhead 8–12% Project management, site supervision, temporary facilities, insurance, and contractor margin. Legitimate general contractors are not hiding their overhead — it is the cost of professional project management.

What moves the number significantly

Within the ranges above, a handful of factors have an outsized effect on where your project lands. These are the decisions that move costs by tens of thousands of dollars, not hundreds.

Lot conditions and site complexity

Toronto lot conditions vary enormously. A flat, rectangular lot with good access and municipal services at the property line is the cheapest scenario. A ravine-adjacent lot, a property with mature trees in the footprint, an infill lot with tight access, or a site that requires underpinning of a neighbouring foundation can each add $100K to $400K to the project cost before the house itself gets any more expensive. Always have a site assessment done before finalizing your budget.

Basement depth and configuration

Most Toronto custom homes include a fully finished basement, and this adds meaningfully to the cost. A standard 8.5-foot basement ceiling requires deeper excavation than the old standard, and many clients are now specifying 9-foot or 10-foot basement ceilings to accommodate home theatres, gym spaces, or legal secondary suites. Each foot of additional basement depth costs roughly $15K to $25K in added excavation and concrete work for a typical Toronto footprint.

Building envelope specification

The Ontario Building Code sets minimum standards, and those minimums are not where you want to be on a custom home you intend to own for decades. Better insulation, triple-pane windows, and a tighter air barrier add to upfront cost but reduce energy bills, improve comfort, and increase the durability of the structure. The payback period on a better envelope on a Toronto custom home is typically 8 to 14 years in energy savings alone, before accounting for the comfort and resale value benefits.

Kitchen and bathroom count and complexity

A 3,000 sq ft house with one kitchen, three bathrooms, and a powder room will cost meaningfully less than the same square footage with two kitchens, five bathrooms, and an ensuite designed around a freestanding tub and custom double vanity. The per-square-foot average obscures this completely. Count your wet rooms and think carefully about their specifications early in the budget process.

On square footage itself: Not all square footage is equal in cost. The most expensive square footage in any house is usually the ground floor, where kitchens, primary bathrooms, and the main living areas tend to cluster. Adding square footage as a second storey over an existing footprint is generally cheaper per square foot than expanding the footprint. A smaller, more thoughtfully designed house almost always delivers more value than a larger one with the same budget stretched thinner.

How Toronto compares to the GTA and Muskoka

Construction labour costs are relatively consistent across the Toronto CMA, but there are meaningful differences between building in the city core versus the outer suburbs or cottage country.

  • Toronto (416): Higher site complexity, tighter access, more demanding permit and zoning requirements, and premium land costs. Expect to spend more time and money on the pre-construction phase relative to total project cost.
  • Inner suburbs (905 — Mississauga, Oakville, Richmond Hill, Markham): Easier site access and often less regulatory complexity than Toronto proper. Development charges can actually be higher in some 905 municipalities than in Toronto, which surprises many clients. Build costs are similar to Toronto within 5–10%.
  • Muskoka: Cottage and recreational property construction comes with its own cost pressures: longer material delivery distances, seasonal constraints on construction windows, septic and well systems instead of municipal services, and structural requirements for Muskoka's freeze-thaw cycles. Budget $450 to $750 per square foot for a well-specified Muskoka build, with site and servicing costs on top.

The soft cost layer that most budgets underestimate

Soft costs deserve their own section because they are consistently underbudgeted by clients doing preliminary feasibility work. On a typical Toronto custom home project, soft costs include:

  • Architectural fees: Full design and construction administration typically runs 8–12% of construction cost on a custom home. On a $1.5M build, that is $120K to $180K. Some architects charge fixed fees; others work on percentage. Understand which model you are dealing with from the start.
  • Structural, mechanical, and civil engineering: Required for permit and often for specialized systems. Budget $15K to $45K depending on project complexity.
  • Toronto permit fees: The City of Toronto charges permit fees based on construction value. For a custom home with a declared construction value of $1.5M, expect permit fees in the range of $25K to $40K. Complex projects take 4 to 12 months to receive a building permit in Toronto — this wait time is real and affects your schedule.
  • Development charges: Toronto levies development charges on new residential construction that partially fund infrastructure. As of 2026, development charges for a new single-family home in Toronto total approximately $100K to $130K. This often shocks clients who have never built new before.
  • Survey, geotechnical, and environmental reports: Depending on lot conditions, you may need a topographic survey, boundary survey, soils report, and in some cases an environmental site assessment. Budget $5K to $20K.
  • Legal fees: Closing costs if buying land, title insurance, and legal review of contracts. Budget $8K to $20K.

On development charges specifically: Development charges in Toronto have increased substantially in recent years and are now a material budget item on any new build. They are payable at permit issuance, not at project completion, which means you need this cash available early. Some clients are surprised to learn they have a six-figure bill before a single shovel goes in the ground. Do not leave this out of your feasibility math.

Timeline: what to expect from start to move-in

Cost and timeline are closely related on a custom home project. A longer project means more months of financing costs, more months of carrying existing housing costs if you have sold, and more exposure to material price fluctuations. Here is a realistic timeline for a Toronto custom home in 2026.

Phase Typical Duration Notes
Design & permit drawings 3–6 months Concept design, schematic design, design development, and construction documents. The more thorough this phase, the fewer surprises during construction.
Toronto permit review 4–12 months The City of Toronto's building permit review is the biggest wild card in the schedule. Complex projects, infill lots, and projects requiring zoning variances take longer. Plan for the long end.
Demolition & site prep 2–6 weeks Includes asbestos abatement if existing structure is pre-1990, demolition, and site clearing.
Foundation & structure 3–5 months Excavation, concrete work, waterproofing, structural framing, roof structure. Weather-dependent.
Rough mechanical & envelope 2–4 months Rough plumbing, HVAC, and electrical; insulation, windows and doors, roofing.
Finishes & fixtures 4–7 months Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, painting, trim, fixtures. This phase requires the most active client decision-making and selection oversight.
Completion & occupancy 4–8 weeks Final inspections, occupancy permit, deficiency corrections, and final cleaning.

Total time from starting the design process to moving in is typically 18 to 30 months for a Toronto custom home. Projects with complex zoning situations, larger square footage, or higher specification levels tend toward the longer end. Clients who start the process expecting to be in their home in 12 months are almost always disappointed.

How to pressure-test a builder's estimate

When you receive a cost estimate from a custom home builder, a few things will tell you quickly whether the number is credible.

  • Is the specification level documented? A credible estimate includes a detailed scope or specification sheet that defines exactly what is included in each line item. "Kitchen allowance: $50,000" with no further detail is not an estimate you can rely on. Ask for a specification that tells you what that $50K is expected to cover.
  • Are exclusions clearly listed? A properly structured estimate lists what is not included as explicitly as what is. If the estimate does not have an exclusions section, the scope ambiguity will cost you money later.
  • Does it include a contingency? Any honest estimate on a custom home in Toronto includes a contingency line item of 8–12% for unforeseen conditions. A builder who presents a number with no contingency is either inexperienced or presenting a number that will grow. In older Toronto neighbourhoods, there is almost always something unexpected below grade or behind walls when you are doing demolition adjacent to an existing structure.
  • Has the estimator visited the site? Cost estimates produced without a site visit are not reliable for anything other than the very roughest of feasibility checks. Lot conditions matter too much to price from an address alone.
  • Are the soft costs included or excluded? Clarify explicitly whether permits, development charges, engineering fees, and HST are included or excluded from the number being presented. Getting apples-to-apples comparisons between builders requires this clarity.

Conclusion: build your budget from the ground up

The per-square-foot figure is a useful shorthand, but it is only the beginning of understanding what your custom home will actually cost. A realistic Toronto custom home budget in 2026 starts with an honest assessment of your site conditions, a clear specification level, and a full accounting of soft costs, HST, and financing. For most clients, the all-in number is meaningfully larger than the construction-only figure suggests.
