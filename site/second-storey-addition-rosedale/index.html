Why Rosedale clients choose Kopman Build

A second storey addition in Rosedale is one of the most technically and contextually demanding projects in Toronto residential construction. The neighbourhood's housing stock — much of it built between the 1890s and 1930s — carries heritage value, unique structural characteristics, and an architectural language that any addition must respect. Kopman Build works with architects experienced in this context and manages the full permit and heritage approval process in-house.

Rosedale's ravine lots add another layer of constraint: TRCA permitting, top-of-bank setbacks, and stormwater management are real considerations even when a project only adds height. We assess every site constraint before a single drawing is produced, so clients enter the process with an accurate picture of cost, timeline, and approvals.

Finish expectations in Rosedale are consistently high. Kopman Build has supplied and installed the calibre of millwork, stone, and hardware these homes require — and our supplier relationships reflect that scope of work. Every project is managed by Dustin Kopman directly, with on-site supervision throughout construction.

Questions about second storey additions in Rosedale

How much does a second storey addition cost in Rosedale?

A second storey addition in Rosedale typically runs $450,000–$900,000+ depending on scope, structural complexity, and finish level. Rosedale homes from the 1890s–1930s often require additional structural engineering to support a new upper floor, and clients in the neighbourhood consistently expect high-end finishes. Kopman Build provides a detailed line-item estimate before any commitment.

Do Rosedale homes have heritage restrictions that affect second storey additions?

Some Rosedale properties are individually listed or located within a Heritage Conservation District, which means the City of Toronto will review additions for compatibility with the existing character. This does not prevent a second storey addition, but it does require thoughtful design and heritage impact assessments. Kopman Build has experience navigating Toronto's heritage approval process and works with architects who specialize in this context.

How do ravine lot setbacks affect a second storey addition in Rosedale?

Rosedale has a high proportion of ravine-adjacent lots governed by the Toronto Ravine and Natural Feature Protection (RNFP) By-law and TRCA permitting requirements. While a second storey addition generally adds height rather than footprint, the overall project may trigger TRCA review if other work is proposed near the top-of-bank. We assess all site constraints during the pre-construction phase and coordinate with TRCA where required.

How long does a second storey addition take in Rosedale?

From initial consultation to occupancy, a second storey addition in Rosedale typically takes 14–20 months. Permitting alone can take 4–8 months, and heritage or Committee of Adjustment applications can add further time. Construction itself runs 8–14 months. Kopman Build gives clients a realistic end-to-end timeline from day one so there are no surprises.
