Maccabi Partners is a joint venture between Gord Holdings and Kopman Holdings. It backs technology that makes established industries better rather than technology that tries to end them — starting with the one industry both founders can see from the inside.

01  ·  Position

The sentence

Read firstThis document proposes a position, not a fact pattern. Everything in it is a founders' draft for discussion between Michael Gord and Dustin Kopman. No figure here has been audited and no entity described has been formed.

Maccabi Partners buys and builds ordinary businesses, and makes them exceptional with technology that never threatens them.

That sentence is doing three jobs at once, and each is deliberate.

"Ordinary businesses" — construction, property, trades, services. Real companies with real revenue, in industries nobody calls exciting. This is where the founders' operating experience actually lives, and it is the opposite of where most technology capital goes.

"Technology that never threatens them" — software built to raise an operator's margin, not to disintermediate the operator. The technology serves the business. Where most venture capital funds the company trying to replace the incumbent, Maccabi funds the incumbent and gives it the tools.

"Buys and builds" — permanent ownership, not a fund with a clock. The vehicle is a joint venture between two family offices. It has no exit date because it does not need one.

02  ·  Refinement

What we would change
about the idea

The concept as described is sound. Five refinements make it defensible in a room with people who do this professionally.

R — 01

Stop calling it a private equity firm

As described: "a private equity vehicle focused on non-disruptive technologies."

It is not a private equity fund and it should not present as one. There is no blind pool, no limited partners, no management fee, no carry, and no fund term. It is a joint-venture holding company between two family offices — permanent capital, deployed at the principals' discretion.

Three reasons the distinction matters. It is true, and sophisticated readers detect the difference instantly. It is stronger — permanent capital with no clock is a genuine advantage over sponsors who must exit in five years, and operators being acquired care about that more than about brand. And it is cleaner: a public website describing a fund is marketing securities, which brings Ontario securities regulation into scope in a way that describing a holding company does not.

Use "private equity" to describe the discipline. Use "joint venture" and "permanent capital" to describe the structure.

R — 02

"Non-disruptive" is a real school of thought — claim the lineage

As described: non-disruptive technologies, as a category preference.

Non-disruptive creation is not a coinage. It is the thesis of Beyond Disruption (W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, Harvard Business Review Press, 2023) — the Blue Ocean authors' argument that innovation can create new markets and new jobs without displacing industries, companies or workers, and that the displacement most innovators treat as a cost of doing business is in fact optional.

Anchoring to a named, peer-reviewed body of work does something no amount of positioning copy can: it gives a small firm an intellectual position that is larger than its balance sheet. Maccabi is not inventing a category. It is applying an established one to industries the authors never wrote about.

It also supplies the moral argument, which matters more than finance people admit. Disruption in the trades means putting builders out of work. Non-disruptive technology means the same builder, better equipped.

R — 03

The rollup is the most crowded trade in North American private equity

As described: the private equity vehicle behind the Kopman Build rollup.

Add-on acquisitions were 75.9% of all US buyout activity in Q2 2025 — above the five-year average of 72.5% and still risingReported. The specific sectors named as most active in 2026 include HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical contracting and landscaping. Every one of those is a construction trade.

So "we are rolling up trades" is not a differentiator; it is table stakes, and Maccabi will bid against sponsors with more capital and dedicated deal teams. The differentiation has to be somewhere else, and it is:

  • The operator is the sponsor. Most rollups are financial buyers acquiring operators they will never be able to run. Here the operating company came first, and has been building since 1999.
  • The software is built in-house, on the group's own sites, before it is sold to anyone. It is not a portfolio-wide ERP bolted on after close.
  • The capital is permanent. A seller handing over a business they spent thirty years building would rather sell to someone who is not required to flip it.

Lead with alignment. Never lead with the strategy.

R — 04

Say plainly that it is small, and make that the point

As described: "currently small but hopefully one day big."

That instinct is right and should survive into the public copy. Emerging managers lose credibility by writing like established ones — the language of a firm with billions under management, attached to a firm with one operating relationship, reads as pretence and is recognised immediately.

The honest version is stronger: two family offices, one operating platform, and a thesis we intend to prove on our own capital before we ever manage anyone else's. That sentence is disarming, unfalsifiable, and true. It also makes the first outside investor's decision easy to frame, because by then there will be a record instead of a projection.

R — 05

Separate what Maccabi holds from what Maccabi buys

As described: Maccabi holds equity in both Kopman companies, and also takes ownership in positions from Michael's portfolio so that it has experience across multiple sectors — with the primary go-to-market remaining non-disruptive markets.

Contributing portfolio positions is the right move and it solves a real problem. It gives the vehicle sector breadth on day one, a mark-to-market record that is not a single construction business, and something to point at when an owner asks what else Maccabi has seen. It converts the concentration risk from fatal to manageable.

But it introduces a different exposure, and it is one sophisticated readers will find immediately: if Maccabi holds positions in technology businesses that win by displacing incumbents, does the non-disruptive thesis mean anything?

The answer is clean, but only if it is said out loud and structurally: Maccabi has a held book and a forward mandate, and they are not the same thing. The held book is what was contributed at formation — diversified, multi-sector, not chosen against the thesis because it predates it. The forward mandate is what Maccabi originates from here, and it is non-disruptive without exception.

Firms are given considerable latitude on a seeded book and none at all on a mandate that drifts. Publish the distinction rather than letting someone find it — a contributed position explained is a track record; a contributed position discovered is a contradiction.

03  ·  Structure

Who owns what,
and why it aligns

Two family offices, one shared vehicle, and a deliberate crossing of interests that did not exist before.

Open items — resolve before formation

Four things this diagram cannot yet state, and each needs an answer in writing before any of it becomes public:

The ownership split of Maccabi between the two holdings companies. The size of the stakes Maccabi takes in Kopman Build and Kopman Properties, and whether those stakes are purchased, contributed or earned. Which of Gord Holdings' portfolio positions are contributed, at what valuation, and whether they come across as equity or as a capital account. What each family receives in exchange for what it puts in — the two contributions are not naturally equal and the split should reflect that, not split the difference. And whether GDA Group itself sits inside or outside the arrangement, which determines whether Maccabi ever competes with it for a deal.

A joint venture between friends is easiest to paper when nobody needs it papered.

04  ·  Thesis

Non-disruptive,
precisely defined

LineageW. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne, Beyond Disruption: Innovate and Achieve Growth without Displacing Industries, Companies, or Jobs. Harvard Business Review Press, 2023. The same authors' earlier work is Blue Ocean Strategy.

Disruption creates value by destroying something. A disruptive company wins share that belonged to an incumbent; the gain on one side of the ledger is a loss on the other, and the loss lands on real firms and real employment. That trade is often worth making, and it is the basis of most technology investing. It is also, as a matter of arithmetic, only one of two available paths.

Non-disruptive creation is the other. It opens markets that did not exist, or lifts the productivity of markets that already do, without taking share from anyone. Nobody's business is ended for the new value to appear.

Maccabi applies that idea to a narrow and unglamorous field: software and systems that make established operating businesses measurably better at what they already do. Estimating, scheduling, cost intelligence, procurement, quality control, client management. Not marketplaces that disintermediate the contractor. Not platforms that turn a trade into a commodity. Tools the operator owns and the operator's margin absorbs.

Two vehicles, one spectrum

This is also why Maccabi does not compete with GDA Group for a single deal. GDA's stated position is global capital for disruptive technology. Maccabi's is its exact complement. Between them, Gord Holdings covers the whole innovation spectrum without either vehicle ever bidding against the other.

The name

The Maccabees restored. They did not replace.

The etymology of Maccabee is genuinely contested. The most widely held reading traces it to the Aramaic maqqəḇa — a hammer — for Judah's conduct in battle. Others read it as an acronym of Mi kamokha ba'elim Adonai, or as a form meaning "the one designated." Scholarship has not settled it, and any brand story built on the name should say so rather than pick the convenient version.

What is not contested is what the Maccabees did when they won: they cleaned and rededicated a building that already stood. The festival that commemorates it, Hanukkah, is named for that dedication rather than for the victory. A hammer is a builder's tool before it is a weapon — and restoring rather than replacing is, two thousand years early, the thesis of this firm.

05  ·  Mandate

What Maccabi holds,
and what it buys

Two books, two different rules. Conflating them is how a thesis stops meaning anything.

Book one — held

The contributed portfolio

Positions contributed at formation: equity in the Kopman operating companies from Kopman Holdings, and a selection of existing positions from Gord Holdings' portfolio across other sectors.

Purpose: sector breadth, a diversified starting balance sheet, and a real record from day one rather than a single operating relationship. These are held and managed. They were not selected against the forward mandate, because they predate it.

Book two — forward

The origination mandate

Everything Maccabi goes out and acquires from here. This is the firm's actual go-to-market, the thing it says yes and no to, and the only book its reputation is built on.

Rule: non-disruptive, without exception. Owner-operated businesses and the technology that makes them better. The criteria below govern this book and only this book.

How to say it

"Maccabi was seeded with positions from both families — that is where the sector breadth comes from, and we are glad to have it. What we originate is non-disruptive: established businesses and the technology that improves them. If you are asking what we will buy next, the second answer is the one that matters."

Criteria for the forward mandate

Generic criteria describe roughly sixty per cent of middle-market vehicles. These are written to exclude.

We invest in
  • Owner-operated businesses in construction, property, trades and adjacent services
  • Companies with real revenue and real customers — not pre-revenue technology
  • Situations where a software or systems layer measurably raises the operator's margin
  • Greater Toronto Area and Southern Ontario, where the founders can be on site
  • Sellers who care where their business and their people end up
  • Control or meaningful minority positions, held without a defined exit date
We do not invest in
  • Technology whose business model requires an existing industry to shrink
  • Marketplaces that disintermediate the operator we would otherwise back
  • Pre-revenue or pre-product companies — that is GDA Group's remit, not this one
  • Anything requiring a five-year exit to work
  • Businesses neither principal could stand in front of and explain
  • Sectors where Maccabi would be bidding against a dedicated sponsor with a deal team

The value-creation model

Four levers, in the order they are pulled. The discipline is that nothing in the fourth column is underwritten at entry.

LeverWhat it isWho executesUnderwritten?
01 · Acquire wellBuy owner-operated businesses at private-market multiples, in a market where sellers value continuity over headline priceMaccabiYes
02 · Operate betterApply the group's existing estimating, scheduling and cost discipline — the systems Kopman Build already runs onKopman GroupYes
03 · Equip with softwareDeploy BuildOS across acquired operators; the tooling is built and proven internally before it is rolled outKopman GroupPartly
04 · Re-rate at scaleMultiple expansion from consolidating small operators into one professionalised platformMarketNever

Multiple arbitrage is the most cited driver of buy-and-build returns and the least controllable. It should be an outcome the vehicle enjoys, never an assumption it depends on. Any deal that only works on lever four is a deal Maccabi declines.

06  ·  Governance

The conflicts,
written down first

A vehicle owned by two families, investing in one family's operating companies, employing the other family's other vehicle in adjacent markets. The conflicts are structural. They are also entirely manageable if named early.

Related-party pricing

Maccabi will own stakes in companies that transact with each other and with their own shareholders. Every internal contract needs a written scope and an external benchmark. If Kopman Build performs work for a Maccabi-owned company at a preferential rate, that is a transfer between shareholders, and it must be visible as one.

Allocation between vehicles

If an opportunity is both disruptive and adjacent to construction, does it go to GDA or Maccabi? Write the rule now, while it is hypothetical. The natural line is the thesis itself: if it wins by displacing an incumbent, it is not a Maccabi deal.

Time and attention

Both principals have day jobs running other things. A vehicle that depends on two part-time founders will move at the speed of the busier one. Decide what each owes the venture in hours and in decision rights, and decide it before a deal is on a clock.

Exit and deadlock

Two-party joint ventures deadlock. A shotgun clause, a valuation mechanic and a tag-along are unglamorous to negotiate at formation and impossible to negotiate at the point they are needed. This is the single most valuable hour of legal work the venture will buy.

Regulatory posture

While Maccabi invests only the principals' own capital, it is a holding company and its website is corporate information. The moment it accepts outside capital it is distributing securities in Ontario, and prospectus, registration and marketing rules apply to everything already published. Get Canadian securities counsel before the first outside dollar — not after.

Serial acquisition scrutiny

Competition regulators in North America have begun treating strings of individually small acquisitions as reviewable in aggregate where they build local dominance. At current scale this is remote. At the scale the venture is aiming for, in a single metropolitan trade market, it is foreseeable and worth tracking.

Handle these two numbers carefully

The IRR comparison is the most quoted statistic in buy-and-build and the most misused. The underlying study covers predominantly European transactions exited between 1998 and 2012. It is old, geographically narrow, and predates both the current interest-rate environment and the crowding described in Refinement 03. Cite it as evidence that the strategy has historically worked, never as a forecast of what this vehicle will earn — and never in any document that could be read as an offer.

ReadingThe same sources that support the strategy also describe its crowding. Both belong in the narrative. A thesis that only cites its own tailwinds is a marketing document.

The mechanics behind those returns are consistent across sources: buying small operators at lower multiples and selling a consolidated platform at higher ones; procurement, overhead and density economics that only appear at scale; and the professionalisation of management, pricing and systems in businesses that never had them.

All three are available to Maccabi. Only the third is fully within its control, and it happens to be the one the founders are actually equipped to execute — because one of them has been running the professionalised version of exactly these businesses since 1999.

The honest read of the evidence is therefore: the strategy works, the strategy is crowded, and the third lever is where a small operator-led vehicle can still win.

08  ·  Candour

What has to be true

Risk 01 — Concentration, and the cure's side effect

Contributing positions from Gord Holdings' portfolio materially reduces the original problem: Maccabi is no longer one operator, one sector and one city. That is the right fix and it should be taken.

What replaces it is subtler. A vehicle whose held book spans several sectors and whose forward mandate is narrow has to explain itself twice — once for what it owns and once for what it buys. Handled with the two-book framing in Sheet 05 it is a strength. Left unstated, the first sharp reader concludes the thesis is decoration. And the operating concentration does not disappear: Kopman Group is still the only business either principal can actually influence day to day.

Risk 02 — The software is a promise, not a product

BuildOS is real code, but it has not yet run a full year of the group's own projects, and it has never been deployed into an acquired company. Lever three of the value-creation model is unproven. Underwrite deals as though it does not exist, and treat it as upside until a second operator is live on it.

Risk 03 — Two part-time principals

Neither founder is doing this full time. Proprietary deal flow in a crowded market is a full-time job, and the vehicle will lose competitive processes to sponsors with dedicated teams. The answer is not to compete on speed — it is to buy only where the relationship, not the process, produced the opportunity.

Risk 04 — Permanent capital cuts both ways

No clock means no forced sales, and it also means no forced discipline. Funds are compelled to mark, report and exit. A family joint venture can carry an underperforming asset indefinitely because nobody makes it stop. Adopt a reporting cadence and a written impairment trigger voluntarily, or the advantage becomes the flaw.

Risk 05 — The name carries meaning that is not yours to spend

Maccabi is a name with deep and specific significance, and it is already borne by institutions worldwide. Using it commits the firm to conduct that can stand beside it, and it is worth confirming there is no trademark conflict in the relevant classes. Chosen deliberately it is an asset. Chosen casually it is a liability that compounds.

The one-liner and the pitch

One-liner

For an introduction

"We buy good, boring businesses and give them technology that makes them better instead of technology that puts them out of work."

Thirty seconds

Spoken — read it aloud before using it

Message house

One core message, three pillars, and the proof each pillar rests on. Every piece of communication traces to one of these three — anything that does not is off-message and should be cut.

Core message

Technology should make good businesses better, not make them obsolete.

Pillar 01 — Aligned

The operator and the capital are the same people. We are not a fund that bought a builder; we are a builder that raised a fund alongside it.

Proof: Kopman Group, operating since 1999. BuildOS, built on the group's own sites. Both principals invest their own capital.

Pillar 02 — Non-disruptive

We back the incumbent. Our technology raises the operator's margin; it never depends on the operator's decline.

Proof: the published mandate excluding disintermediating models. The intellectual lineage in Beyond Disruption. GDA Group holds the disruptive mandate separately.

Pillar 03 — Permanent

No fund term, no forced exit, no clock. We can hold a business for as long as it deserves to be held.

Proof: family-office capital, joint-venture structure, no third-party limited partners and no defined term — and a balance sheet seeded from both houses across several sectors.

10  ·  Voice

How the firm sounds

Always
  • Plain, short, declarative. The register is a letter from an operator, not a memorandum from a bank.
  • Concrete nouns. Loading docks, estimating software, job sites, payroll. Never "solutions," "synergies" or "value creation opportunities."
  • Numbers with provenance or no numbers at all.
  • Admit the size. Small and honest beats large and unverifiable.
  • Name the people. Firms of this size are their principals.
Never
  • Any target return, projection or performance figure in public material. This is a legal line, not a stylistic one.
  • "Fund," "LP," "AUM" or "carry" until they are true.
  • Borrowed institutional grandeur — no unstaffed offices, no illustrative statistics, no logos the firm has not earned.
  • Disruption language. It is the opposite of the thesis and reads as unexamined.
  • Anything that could be read as an offer to sell a security.

The whole document in one paragraph

Maccabi Partners is a permanent-capital joint venture between two family offices, not a private equity fund, and should say so. It backs established operating businesses and the technology that improves them rather than replaces them — a thesis with a real intellectual lineage and a precise complement to GDA Group's disruptive mandate. The rollup strategy it will pursue is the most crowded in North American private equity, so it must lead with alignment rather than strategy: the operator is the sponsor, the software is built in-house, and the capital has no clock.
