Sheet 03.2  —  The core move

One family, three temperatures

Build sells craft to homeowners; its warmth is correct and should not change. Properties sells industrial competence to brokers and tenants, and warm linen reads soft in that room. The differentiation is carried entirely by ground temperature and accent — type, near-black, hairline discipline and layout logic stay constant.

kopman.group

Neutral authority

The parent is the quietest of the three. Near-neutral paper, no arm-specific accent, the most white space and the least imagery. It exists to look unmistakably permanent — a company that owns other companies, not one that is selling you something on this page.

kopmanbuild.com

Warm — unchanged

Linen ground, terracotta accent, editorial serif. This is a residential craft brand and it already works. The only changes proposed for Build are corrections, not redesign — listed in Sheet 03.8.

kopman.properties

Cold — new

Ground shifts from linen to poured concrete: the yellow is pulled out, a trace of grey pushed in. Terracotta is replaced by dock amber — the ochre of loading-bay markings — used only for signal, never decoration. A cold steel blue joins as a second data axis for charts and comparables.

Kopman Properties — full token set

Dark theme is mandatory, not optional. Brokers and investors read on phones at night. Ground inverts to #141517, panels to #1E2124, amber lifts to #D2932F and steel to #7FA3B8 to hold contrast. Define every colour as a token on :root and redefine only the tokens in the dark blocks — never declare a colour for the first time inside a media query.

Sheet 03.5  —  Information architecture

What each site is made of

kopman.group — small, dense, permanent

/ · position, the arms, the numbers, leadership, contact — one page, seven sections
/group/ · structure, ownership, history from 1999
/leadership/ · named people with real bios — the highest-value page on the site
/build/  /properties/ · one page per arm, each linking out to its own domain
/buildos/ · the software arm
/news/ · acquisitions, completions, appointments — the entity-signal engine
/contact/ · one real address, one real phone number

Roughly eight pages. Resist growth. A parent site that starts publishing service pages is competing with its own children — see Sheet 04. Its job is to be the entity anchor and to look like it has been there for twenty-six years, because it has.

kopman.properties — a thesis, then the evidence

/ · the thesis in one screen: the gap, the retrofit, the standard
/thesis/ · the long-form argument, with sourced market data
/standard/ · the Kopman retrofit specification — what every asset gets
/portfolio/ · index of assets
/portfolio/<asset>/ · per-asset: title block, dock schedule, before/after, specs
/available/ · space for lease or sale — the transactional page
/available/<submarket>/ · Scarborough, Brampton, Mississauga, Burlington…
/insights/ · the obsolescence data nobody else publishes
/sell-your-building/ · vendor acquisition — the off-market sourcing page
/about/  /contact/

/sell-your-building/ is easy to overlook and may be the commercially most valuable page on the site. The scarce input in this business is off-market obsolete stock, and the owner of a tired warehouse who wants out is searching for someone to call. Give them one.

Two audiences, one site — the split that matters

Properties serves two readers who want opposite things, and the design must not average them:

Reader A — the tenant or buyer

Wants square footage, clear height, dock count, power, drive time to the 401, and a phone number. Impatient, often on a phone, often forwarded a link by a broker. Their path must be: land → specs → contact, in under three screens. No thesis, no narrative.

Reader B — the vendor, broker or investor

Wants evidence that Kopman can actually execute: completed retrofits, real cost data, sourced market analysis, named people. Patient, reads long-form, returns more than once. Their path is: thesis → standard → portfolio → contact.

The homepage must fork visibly within the first screen — one route labelled for space, one for the thesis. A single blended homepage serves neither.

Sheet 03.6  —  Imagery

Photography direction

This is the area where a property site most often fails, and the one where Kopman currently has the least in hand. Build's site references an og.jpg that does not exist on the server; every social and messaging preview across all 630 of its pages currently renders blank. Properties cannot repeat that.

Photograph the unglamorous condition honestly

Cracked apron, rusted grade door, ponded roof, the exact wall where the dock will be cut. Buyers of value-add do not want to see a pretty building — they want to see that the seller understood what was wrong with it. Honest "before" imagery is the single strongest credibility signal available to this brand.

Fix the camera position and return to it

Mark the position on the site plan at acquisition and shoot from it at every milestone. A true fixed-position sequence is impossible to fake and reads instantly as real.

Shoot in flat overcast light

Industrial buildings photograph badly in hard sun — the yard blows out and the dock wall goes black. Overcast reads factual, and factual is the brand.

Include a truck at the dock in at least one frame per asset

It is the proof photograph. It shows the retrofit works at the only scale that matters.

Never use stock warehouse photography

Brokers recognise it immediately, and a stock photograph of someone else's building on a page about your building is the fastest way to lose a vendor's trust.

Never publish a rendering that could be mistaken for a photograph

Label renderings inside the image. A rendering passed off as a completed building is a misrepresentation a broker will remember permanently.

Never ship a page without a real Open Graph image

Every asset page needs its own 1200×630 OG image showing that specific building. This is how the page appears when a broker pastes it into email — the most common way it will ever be seen.

Sheet 03.7  —  Motion & access

Restraint and reach

Motion

Build's existing treatment — a single IntersectionObserver reveal at 0.08 threshold — is correct and carries over unchanged. Properties adds exactly one motion idea: on an asset page, the before/after pair cross-fades on scroll or on drag. Nothing else moves.

Institutional readers distrust animated property sites; motion reads as sales pressure. Honour prefers-reduced-motion — currently absent from Build's stylesheet and required on both new sites.

Accessibility — non-negotiable

  • 4.5:1 minimum on body text in both themes. Amber #A96C14 on concrete clears it; the lighter dark-theme amber is specified precisely to hold it.
  • Amber must never be the only carrier of meaning — pair it with a label or border, since a dock diagram in amber alone excludes colour-blind readers.
  • Visible :focus-visible on every control. Brokers use keyboards.
  • Every data table gets real <th> scope and an overflow-x wrapper so the page body never scrolls sideways.
  • Minimum body size 17px. Property specs are frequently read on a phone in a parking lot.

Sheet 03.9  —  Build spec

How to build it

kopman.group

Static, hand-authored, no framework — matching Build's approach, which is genuinely well-suited to an eight-page site. Eight files, one stylesheet, no build step. It will still be correct in ten years, which is the point of a parent-company site.

kopman.properties

Next.js with a content layer. Asset pages are data-driven — one record per building generates the title block, dock schedule, spec table, OG image and JSON-LD. This is the one place a framework earns its cost, because the same asset data must render as a web page, a PDF brochure and structured data.

Shared, mandatory on both

  • Token-based theming with light, dark and system all handled — no colour declared for the first time inside a media query.
  • Per-page generated OG images. No shared fallback, and never a missing file.
  • Entity schema wired on day one: parentOrganization, subOrganization, matching sameAs across all three domains.
  • A real form endpoint, with server-side storage and an email notification, verified by a live test submission before launch. BuildOS already contains the lead capture to receive it.
  • Analytics and call tracking from the first deploy — instrumentation added later cannot measure the launch it was meant to evaluate.
