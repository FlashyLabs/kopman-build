The opening thesis survives contact with the numbers — but not in the form it was proposed. The money is not in charging more rent for a dock. It is in turning one unleasable building into six leasable bays.

Sheet 02.0  —  Read this first

How to read the numbers

Two research desks were run for this plan — one on the GTA industrial market and live listings, one on Ontario retrofit construction costs. Both hit the same wall: this environment's network policy blocked direct access to every primary source — CBRE, Colliers, JLL, LoopNet, Realtor.ca, municipal by-law PDFs, Altus. Search-engine synthesis worked; opening the underlying document did not.

Provenance rule

Nothing in this document has been read at its source by the team that assembled it. Every figure is marked Source — appeared in search results attributed to a named publisher — or Derived — our own arithmetic or estimating judgement. There is deliberately no "verified" tier.

No figure here should enter an investment committee memo until it has been re-pulled from the primary document. Property listings in particular must be re-confirmed with the listing broker: several show signs of being stale, and one carries an MLS prefix suggesting a 2024 vintage.

We would rather hand the board a plan that says exactly which numbers are soft than one that reads cleanly and cannot be defended in a room.

Sheet 02.1  —  The correction

The thesis, revised

The proposition brought to the boardroom was: buy warehouses without loading docks, add docks and ramps, and sell or lease into a wider market. The market research supports the premise. The cost research does not support the mechanism.

Costed properly against Ontario conditions, a six-dock conversion on a 30,000 sf building carries roughly $2.06MDerived of dock and truck-court scope once contingency and soft costs are loaded — about $68.80 per square foot of building, or ~$344,000 per dock position all-in. At a 6.5% exit cap, that spend has to produce a $4.47/sf net rent lift merely to break even.

The plausible rent premium for dock-loaded over grade-only small-bay space in the GTA is $1.50–$3.50/sfDerived — and no published source for it exists anywhere our desks could reach. On rent lift alone, the trade is underwater.

The revision

Docks are not a rent story. They are a leasability story. The value is not created by charging a dock premium on space you could already lease. It is created by converting a building that almost nobody can occupy into one that almost anybody can — and then subdividing it into the unit size the market is actually short of.

Underwrite vacancy-to-occupancy, not rent-to-higher-rent. The dock is the key that unlocks the subdivision; the subdivision is where the margin lives.

The arbitrage, stated precisely

Small buildings already carry the small-bay premium in the ask — $463 to $876/SF. Buying small destroys the trade. Buy 30,000–60,000 sf of obsolete stock at $350–$400/SF, retrofit and demise it into 3,000–8,000 sf bays, and exit into a market visibly clearing at $460–$900/SF. The spread is the business.

Why the stock is stranded

The GTA built almost no small-bay industrial for a generation. Nearly half the shallow-bay inventory predates 1980Source, and new supply went overwhelmingly to big-box distribution. Shallow-bay vacancy has run below overall industrial vacancy since 2017, and the gap widened through the last development cycleSource.

Meanwhile the buildings themselves aged out of usefulness. Modern distribution expects roughly one dock door per 5,000–10,000 sfSource. Buildings at 18–24' clear command materially lower rents than modern stock, and below 18' clear is described as essentially obsolete for logistics useSource. A grade-level-only building cannot take a 53' trailer at all.

The number that does not exist

No published GTA figure for the price or rent discount on grade-level-only versus dock-loaded industrial could be found by either research desk. It is the single input this thesis is most sensitive to, and the market does not publish it.

Two readings. Either the spread is thinner than assumed and nobody has proven it — or the absence is the moat, because pricing it requires paired-comp analysis on messy small assets plus a credible retrofit cost, which is exactly the pair of things an owner-builder has and a fund does not. Commission a paired-comp study from a GTA appraiser before the first close. Treat it as thesis-validation spend, not overhead.

Timing

Rents are roughly 9.7%Derived below the Q2 2024 peak, but sale prices have been flat at ~$355/SF since early 2024. Either cap rates compressed on falling income, or — more likely — vendors have not repriced and there is a bid/ask standoff. Absorption has been positive four straight quarters and active construction is down 52% from its 2023 peak. This reads as a late-stage correction. It is a floor call, not a recovery call, and it rewards patience and off-market sourcing over speed.

Sheet 02.3  —  Operating model

How the group executes

Kopman Group's structure is the reason this plan is credible. A financial buyer must tender the retrofit and accept a contractor's price and a contractor's schedule. Kopman performs it.

The acquisition screen

Applied in this order. A property failing gate 1 or 2 is not repriced, it is dropped.

Gate 1 — Truck court depth

Measure from the proposed dock wall to the property line. Under 100 ft: kill. 100–120 ft: single-trailer only, discount rents. 130 ft is the minimum underwritable. 150–180 ft is good; over 180 ft supports trailer-storage incomeSource. Physical feasibility, not zoning, is the likeliest deal-killer.

Gate 2 — Site coverage

Building area ÷ lot area. Above ~45% there is rarely room for a court. This is why the screen runs before any financial analysis: a cheap building on a tight lot cannot be fixed at any price.

Gate 3 — Wall structure

In precast panel buildings, panels are often individually load-bearing, and cutting a 9-ft opening can require replacing a whole panel rather than framing an opening. A structural walk-through before going firm turns a $25,500 opening into a known quantity or a walk-away.

Gate 4 — Soil

Bore the dock-well footprint during due diligence. A dock well means excavating 300–600 m³ beside a building that may have held solvents or a fuel tank. Failing O.Reg 406/19 characterisation moves disposal from ~$60 to $150–$350/m³ — a $150,000–$300,000 surprise and 8–12 weeks.

Four findings that change the economics

FindingEffect
Gang the docks. A shared depressed well spreads retaining wall across positions — ~45 lf per position versus ~130 lf for an isolated dock.Cost per position falls from $150k–$260k to $85k–$190k. Never build singles.
Development charges are zero. DC Act s.4 exempts enlargement of an existing industrial building by 50% or less. A dock conversion adds no GFA and no change of use.SourceRemoves a line most brokers pencil in. Peel also cut DCs 50% to Nov 2026.
No Record of Site Condition needed. An RSC is triggered by a change to a more sensitive use. Industrial to industrial does not trigger it.SourceSaves $25k–$150k against a typical underwriting.
Never raise a roof. A roof lift costs $25–$70/sf; the rent delta between 18' and 28' clear capitalises to roughly $15–$38/sf.Value-destructive at every point in the range. If clear height is unusable, reprice or walk.

The capital-light alternative

A portable steel yard ramp costs $18,000–$35,000, needs no permit, no site plan approval and no construction, and is chattel that can be depreciated or moved. Against $130,000 for a dock position, it is frequently the better answer where only one or two truck positions are needed. Every deal should be modelled both ways — the ramp case is the downside scenario that keeps a marginal acquisition alive.

The reason is visible in the market data. Asking prices have been flat at ~$355/SF since early 2024 while rents fell for eleven straight quarters. At today's asking prices these buildings imply roughly a 4% capDerived against a reported Class B range of 5.25–6.00%Source. Vendors have not repriced.

The conclusion

Kopman Properties is a merchant developer, not an income landlord. The buyer who pays $460–$900/SF for small-bay GTA industrial is an owner-occupier — a trades business, a distributor, an importer — who is comparing the price against building new or against rent, not against a cap rate. That buyer is the exit.

Which means the product is not a leased building. It is six individually saleable small-bay units, each with its own dock, its own service and its own washroom. Build for the owner-occupier, sell to the owner-occupier, and recycle the capital.

Sheet 02.5  —  Case studies

Three buildings on the market

On photography — read before presenting

The brief asked for before photographs and after renderings. We have not supplied them, and we will not fabricate them. Every listing site was egress-blocked in this environment, so no real photograph of any of these three buildings could be obtained — and a generated image of a real, addressable property that a vendor or broker might see would be a misrepresentation, not a rendering.

What follows instead are schematic before/after site plans drawn to the published lot and building dimensions. For board purposes these carry more decision-relevant information than a photograph: they show the dock bank, the court depth and the coverage headroom, which is what the screen actually turns on.

To commission the real imagery, request from each listing broker: (1) the marketing photo set and any drone imagery, (2) the site plan or SRPR, (3) elevation photographs of the proposed dock wall, and (4) permission to shoot. Kopman Build can then produce a genuine rendering from the survey — labelled as a rendering, in-image, per the design brief.

The seasonality trap — model it explicitly

Ontario asphalt plants close from roughly mid-November to mid-April. Cold-weather concrete adds 10–20%, and winter excavation of frozen ground adds more.

If site plan approval lands in September, you cannot pave until May. That is a six-month dead carry that appears in no cost table and is the single largest schedule risk in this plan.

Sequencing rule: target SPA approval for February–March so site works run April to October in one season. Back-solving, that means closing in June–August of the prior year. Acquisition timing is a scheduling decision, not just a pricing one.

Sheet 02.8  —  Go to market

Selling the bays

The product is six owner-occupier units, not a leased building. That changes the entire marketing approach — and it starts long before completion.

Pre-sell from the site plan

Owner-occupiers buy 12–18 months ahead because they are timing a lease expiry. Release units at conditional approval, not at completion. Deposits taken during construction de-risk the exit and remove the single largest assumption in the underwriting — that $600/SF holds.

Syndicate listings, do not fight aggregators

LoopNet, Zolo, CommercialCafe and Spacelist own every transactional GTA industrial search result — Toronto alone shows 373 industrial listings on LoopNet against our handfulSource. They are the distribution channel. Publish to them; win search somewhere else entirely (Sheet 04).

Broker the exit, do not disintermediate it

Small-bay owner-occupiers come through brokers. Pay full commission and pay it visibly — the same brokers are the source of the next off-market acquisition. Kopman Properties needs to be the buyer brokers call first, and that reputation is bought on the sell side.

Sell the specification, not the building

Publish a fixed "Kopman standard" — dock with hydraulic leveller and shelter, dedicated 3-phase service and sub-meter, own washroom, LED throughout, 130 ft court. A repeatable published standard lets a buyer compare against a new-build quote and lets Kopman price consistently across assets.

Open the vendor channel

The scarce input is off-market obsolete stock. The owner of a tired warehouse who wants out is looking for someone to call — and there is no obvious number to call in the GTA today. A /sell-your-building/ page plus direct outreach to long-tenured industrial owners is the cheapest acquisition channel available, and it avoids the bid/ask standoff entirely.

Publish the real numbers

"We bought at $X/SF, spent $Y on docks, sold at $Z" is a genre with essentially zero Canadian supply. It is also the most efficient marketing this group can do — it recruits vendors, brokers and co-investors simultaneously, and it is exactly the content that earns citation in AI search.

Who the buyer is

Named tenant and buyer types for GTA small-bay spaceSource: local service providers, specialty trades, light manufacturers, regional distributors, last-mile operators, e-commerce and direct-to-consumer brands, food and beverage, construction and trades companies, importers and distributors, and event and entertainment companies. The owner-occupier bid is live and documented — cited transactions include a roughly $100M acquisition by Mapei Inc. and a purchase by Forest Contractors Group.

Kopman Build's own client base is a channel. A construction firm operating across the GTA since 1999 knows a great many trades businesses that are currently renting space they have outgrown. That list is an asset no competing developer has.

Sheet 02.10  —  Risk

What breaks this

The exit price is one assumption carrying the whole plan

Every case turns on a $600/SF subdivided exit inferred from seven asking prices, none confirmed at source and at least one probably stale. At $550/SF, Dovedale returns 9.1% before carry — which after financing is roughly break-even. Pre-selling units is the mitigation; commissioning a paired-comp appraisal is the verification. Do both before committing.

Subdivision may need approvals nobody has priced

Selling six units separately means condominium registration or severance — a planning process with its own timeline, cost and refusal risk, and it is not in any number in this document. This is the largest single gap in the plan as it stands. Get a planning opinion before the first LOI.

Contaminated soil at the dock well

Excavating 300–600 m³ beside a former manufacturing building. Failing O.Reg 406/19 characterisation moves disposal from ~$60/m³ to $150–$350/m³ — $150,000–$300,000 and 8–12 weeks. Bore during due diligence, not after close.

Load-bearing precast walls

Where panels carry roof loads individually, a 9-ft opening can mean replacing a panel rather than framing an opening — turning a $25,500 item into a multiple of that, six times over. A structural walk-through before firming is non-negotiable.

Kopman Build has no industrial track record

Residential construction expertise does not transfer to dock design, truck-court geometry, ESFR engineering or industrial leasing. Hire or partner for genuine industrial experience before the first close. It is the largest execution gap in the group.

Internal pricing must be arm's length

If Build absorbs overruns to flatter Properties' returns, the group has relocated a loss, not avoided one — and any future investor will find it in diligence. Written scopes and a market benchmark on every internal contract.

Entering an eleven-quarter rent decline

Absorption has been positive four quarters and construction starts are down 52% from peak, but this is a floor call, not a recovery call. The plan should survive a further 10% rent decline — and on a sell-to-owner-occupier exit it largely does, since that buyer is comparing against replacement cost rather than rent.

The plan in one paragraph

Buy 30,000–60,000 sf of functionally obsolete GTA industrial at $350–$400/SF on lots under 45% coverage. Gang the docks into one shared well, demise into six owner-occupier bays, and sell into a market clearing at $460–$900/SF. Do not hold for income — the income math fails by $180–$320/SF. Do not overpay for the building that best fits the story. And do not start until the exit comp is verified, because a $50/SF move in the exit price is the difference between a 19% margin and a 9% one.
